Alpharetta has quietly become one of the best dining cities in metro Atlanta. Between the chef-driven restaurants of Avalon, the porches of Downtown Alpharetta and the standbys along Windward Parkway, you could eat out every weekend for a year and not repeat yourself. Here are the 15 places locals bring up first.

The Avalon heavy hitters

1. Oak Steakhouse is Alpharetta's special-occasion room: a Charleston-born steakhouse known for dry-aged beef, Wagyu and a serious wine list. Book ahead for weekends, or grab a seat at the bar.

2. South City Kitchen does upscale Southern better than almost anyone: fried chicken, biscuits, shrimp and grits, and one of the area's most popular brunches.

3. Rumi's Kitchen put Avalon on Atlanta's food map with its Persian kabobs, fragrant rice and hummus. If you've never had Persian food, start here.

4. Colletta covers the Italian side with wood-fired pizza and house-made pasta, while 5. Antico Pizza brings Atlanta's most famous Neapolitan pies north of the Perimeter.

6. Superica, Ford Fry's Tex-Mex cantina, is the spot for chorizo queso fundido, fajitas and margaritas, and 7. The Cape adds a Cape Town-inspired rooftop with sunset views and a crab dip people drive in for.

Downtown Alpharetta favorites

8. Smokejack Southern Grill & BBQ has been smoking hand-pulled pork, ribs and burnt ends in its historic brick building on Main Street for years. It's the downtown anchor.

9. Citizen Soul blends California cooking with Southern soul, and 10. The Southern Porch serves fried green tomatoes and biscuits and gravy on an actual screened-in porch.

11. Up On The Roof owns downtown brunch with mimosa carafes and challah French toast above Main Street, while 12. Hen Mother Cookhouse counters with scratch-made comfort food and baked goods for a weekday breakfast.

Beyond the districts

13. Cabernet Steakhouse on Windward Parkway is the old-school, club-like steakhouse Alpharetta regulars swear by, and 14. Connors Steak & Seafood grills prime beef over mesquite with a raw bar and a big bourbon list.

15. Food Terminal rounds out the list with Malaysian street food in the North Point corridor, proof that Alpharetta's dining scene goes well past steak and Southern.

Honorable mentions

You won't go wrong at Delbar or Jerusalem Bakery on Old Milton for Middle Eastern, Nahm for upscale Thai on Windward, Jinya Ramen Bar for ramen downtown, or Maple Street Biscuit Company for a biscuit breakfast downtown. For the full list, browse our Alpharetta restaurant directory.
