Downtown Alpharetta packs more good restaurants into a few walkable blocks than anywhere else in North Fulton. Main Street, Milton Avenue, Roswell Street and the Town Green at City Center hold everything from wood-fired Spanish cooking and dry-aged steaks to rooftop brunch and a beer garden built around food trucks. We checked every restaurant below in November 2026, including current hours and phone numbers, so you can pick a spot and go.

Quick picks

  • Special occasion: Lapeer Steak & Seafood or Little Alley Steak
  • Date night: Foundation Social Eatery or Siena
  • Groups and families: Pure Taqueria, Crust Pasta & Pizzeria or Chiringa
  • Rooftop: Up On The Roof or the rooftop bar at Amasa Mexican Kitchen
  • Breakfast: Hen Mother Cookhouse or The Southern Porch
  • Barbecue and a beer: Smokejack with a pint next door at Currahee Brewing

Steaks and special occasions

Lapeer Steak & Seafood (12 N Broad St) opened in 2019 as a seafood market and was reworked into a steakhouse in 2022, so the menu does both well: a Prime Delmonico ribeye and center-cut filet alongside smoked fish dip, lobster pasta and a warm lobster roll. The room has 15-foot ceilings, an open kitchen and an enclosed patio that looks over City Center. Dinner runs Monday 5pm to 8pm, Tuesday through Thursday until 9pm and Friday and Saturday until 10pm, and it is closed Sundays. Book on OpenTable for weekends.

Little Alley Steak (102 S Main St) took over the longtime Salt Factory Pub space in 2025 as the third location of the Roswell original. Wet-aged and dry-aged steaks are the focus, with oysters and shellfish and a serious wine program. It serves dinner every night from 4pm, closing at 11pm on Fridays and Saturdays, and adds a Sunday lunch from 11am to 3:30pm.

The Butcher and Bottle (45 Roswell St) is a chef-driven steakhouse with Southern roots and handcrafted drinks. It opens at 4pm Monday through Saturday and at 11am on Sunday, closing at 10pm every night.

Date night

Foundation Social Eatery (55 Roswell St) is the one locals name when they want something polished. Chef Mel Toledo and his wife Sandy run it, and the French-inspired menu changes with the seasons while keeping favorites like house-made fettuccine and braised short rib hash. Ask for the chef's table, which looks into the kitchen. Dinner is Monday through Saturday from 5pm, and it is closed Sundays.

Siena (124 Devore Rd), just south of the Main Street core, opened in December 2025 with a Mediterranean and Italian menu built for sharing: whipped feta with burnt honey, crispy halloumi, octopus al ajillo and golden oxtail. A saxophonist plays on Friday nights, there is a free parking lot, and it is dine-in only. Hours are Tuesday through Thursday and Sunday 4pm to 10pm and Friday and Saturday until midnight; it is closed Mondays.

Citizen Soul (60 S Main St) mixes California cooking with Southern heart and pairs it with a wine list that leans on West Coast producers. Book on Resy for Friday and Saturday. It opens at 5pm on weeknights, noon on Saturday and 4pm on Sunday.

Casual dinners and groups

Fogon and Lions (10 Roswell St) cooks Spanish and Latin dishes over a wood-burning fogon, from churrascos and ceviches to whole roasted meats. Chef Julio Delgado is a Food Network "Cutthroat Kitchen" winner, and groups of 10 to 12 can pre-order a whole roasted suckling pig carved at the table. There is a public parking garage right next door. It opens at 4pm Tuesday through Friday and 11am on weekends, and it is closed Mondays.

Pure Taqueria (103 Roswell St) started the whole Pure chain here in 2005, on the site of a 1920s Pure Fuel Oil station. Expect a loud, fun room, tortillas made in house and the margaritas it is known for. It is open daily from 11am, until 10pm on Friday and Saturday.

Amasa Mexican Kitchen (15 Academy St) is the newest big opening downtown, a Baja-inspired spot with a fully gluten-free menu, blue corn tortillas pressed daily, crudos, fish tacos and a rooftop bar with margarita flights. Weekday happy hour runs 4pm to 6pm, and Sunday brings a brunch buffet.

Chiringa (34 S Main St) sits in one of the two small jewel box buildings on the Town Green. Order at the counter, then grab a patio seat facing the green for Baja tacos, a fish sandwich or a cheeseburger with fresh-squeezed cocktails. It is the easy pick before a concert or festival at City Center.

For Italian, Crust Pasta & Pizzeria (131 S Main St) makes its egg pasta, ravioli, pizza dough and even cannoli shells in house and offers a gluten-free crust, while Minnie Olivia Pizzeria (10 Roswell St) fires pizzas in a wood oven inside the historic Dodd Hotel building. Minnie Olivia's dining room is small and closed Mondays, so the patio is your friend. For Louisiana cooking, Flatlands Bourbon & Bayou (52B N Main St) serves po' boys, shrimp and andouille and blackened redfish with a bourbon-heavy bar. And for a bowl of ramen, JINYA Ramen Bar (401 S Broad St) stays open until 11pm on weekends.

Rooftops, beer and late nights

Up On The Roof (33 S Main St) is downtown's best-known rooftop, with small plates, seasonal entrees and views over Main Street. Weekend brunch starts at 11am, and it stays open until 2am on Friday and Saturday. In December it runs holiday events, including a Christmas pop-up bar.

Smokejack Southern Grill & BBQ (29 S Main St) has anchored Main Street for years with hand-pulled pork, ribs and burnt ends in a historic brick building. Next door, Currahee Brewing pours European-style ales and lagers, and you can carry Smokejack's barbecue over to the taproom patio.

Truck & Tap (30 Milton Ave) is a mostly outdoor craft beer bar where a different food truck parks out front each day. It opens at 3pm on weekdays and noon on weekends, and it stays open until 1am on Friday and Saturday. Taffer's Tavern, from Jon Taffer of "Bar Rescue," is another late-night option on Main Street with big booths for groups.

Breakfast and brunch

Hen Mother Cookhouse (50 S Main St) is the downtown breakfast standby for scratch-made comfort food and baked goods. It opens at 7am Tuesday through Friday and 7:30am on weekends, and it is closed Mondays. A few doors up, The Southern Porch (62 N Main St) serves fried green tomatoes and biscuits and gravy on a screened porch every morning.

For a faster stop, Maple Street Biscuit Company (50 Canton St) builds biscuit sandwiches around hand-breaded chicken, and The Nest Cafe (58 Canton St) does waffles, beignets and paninis daily from 7am to 3pm. For a sit-down weekend brunch with cocktails, try Carrie's at The Hamilton hotel (Saturday and Sunday, 11am to 2pm) or the rooftop at Up On The Roof. Our full Alpharetta brunch guide has more.

Parking and getting around

Downtown is built for walking, so park once and stroll. Free public parking decks sit at 92 Milton Avenue and 2 Park Plaza, both a short walk from Main Street, and Fogon and Lions has a public garage right next door. Street spots fill fast on Friday and Saturday nights and during events on the Town Green, so arrive 15 minutes early or head straight for a deck. The paved Alpha Loop connects downtown to Avalon in about a mile if you want to make an evening of both.

Weekend dinner reservations are worth making at Lapeer, Little Alley, Foundation Social Eatery, Siena and Citizen Soul. Most of the casual spots take walk-ins.
