Choosing a financial advisor in Alpharetta: what to know

Alpharetta has a large number of financial advisors, from solo planners to branches of national firms. The title financial advisor is used broadly, so start by learning how a professional is registered and paid. Investment adviser representatives typically have a fiduciary duty when giving investment advice, while brokers sell securities and are held to a different standard of conduct. Some professionals are both. Ask each advisor directly which role they play for you, and ask for their Form CRS, a short disclosure document that explains services, fees and conflicts.

You can check a professional's background for free. FINRA BrokerCheck covers brokers and firms, and the SEC's Investment Adviser Public Disclosure site covers investment advisers, including any disciplinary history. Smaller investment advisers that are not registered with the SEC generally register with the Georgia Secretary of State's Securities Division instead. Look for consistency between what an advisor tells you and what these records show, and take note of any customer complaints or regulatory actions.

In a first meeting, ask how the advisor is compensated, whether through a percentage of assets, flat or hourly fees, commissions or a mix, and what you will pay in total including fund expenses. Ask what services are included, how often you will meet, who holds your assets, and how they handle a market downturn. Red flags include guaranteed returns, pressure to move money quickly, reluctance to put fees in writing, and products you do not understand. This information is general consumer guidance and not financial advice.

Tips for hiring financial advisors in Alpharetta

  • Ask whether the advisor is a fiduciary and how they are paid
  • Compare fee-only, fee-based and commission models
  • Check credentials like CFP and the firm's Form ADV

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the best financial advisors in Alpharetta, GA?

Start with providers based in or regularly serving Alpharetta, check licensing and reviews, and get more than one quote for bigger jobs. This page lists 4 options for retirement planning, investments and wealth management.

How do I check a financial advisor's background?

Search the advisor and firm on FINRA BrokerCheck and the SEC's Investment Adviser Public Disclosure site. Both are free and show registrations and any disciplinary history.

Who regulates financial advisors in Georgia?

Larger investment advisers register with the SEC, while smaller advisers generally register with the Georgia Secretary of State's Securities Division. Brokers are overseen by FINRA.
