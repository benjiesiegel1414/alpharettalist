The four types of senior living, explained

  • 55+ active adult rentals are regular apartments or homes for people 55 and older, with a clubhouse, pool, events and neighbors at the same stage of life. There is no care or meal plan. In Alpharetta they generally rent for about $1,800 to $3,800 a month.
  • Independent living adds services: meals, housekeeping, transportation and a full activity calendar, but no hands-on care.
  • Assisted living adds daily help with things like bathing, dressing and medications, with staff on site around the clock. A Place for Mom puts the average in Alpharetta at about $4,745 a month.
  • Memory care is a secured neighborhood with specially trained staff for people living with Alzheimer's disease or other dementia. It usually costs more than assisted living.

Many communities also offer respite stays, short stays of a few days to a few weeks after a hospital visit or while a family caregiver takes a break. Read our full guide to senior living in Alpharetta for costs, questions to ask and how to pay.

Choosing a senior living community in Alpharetta: what to know

Senior living options around Alpharetta include independent living communities, assisted living communities, smaller personal care homes and memory care. Start with an honest look at the help your loved one needs now and may need in a few years, such as medication reminders, bathing, dressing or supervision for memory loss, because that determines which setting fits. Visit more than once, including at a meal, and talk with residents and families if you can.

Georgia's Department of Community Health, through its Healthcare Facility Regulation Division, licenses personal care homes and assisted living communities. Under state rules, an assisted living community is a personal care home serving 25 or more residents that is licensed to provide assisted living care. The department conducts unannounced inspections roughly every 11 to 15 months and investigates complaints, so ask the community for its license and recent inspection results. Georgia's consumer protection office warns that an unlicensed home advertising for residents violates the Fair Business Practices Act.

Ask about staffing levels on each shift, staff training, how care needs are reassessed, and what happens if needs grow beyond what the community can provide. Get every fee and what triggers a change in writing, and read the residency agreement before signing. Red flags include reluctance to share inspection results, high staff turnover and pressure to sign before you have toured. Georgia's Long Term Care Ombudsman Program advocates for residents of personal care homes and assisted living facilities and can help resolve problems. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Tips for hiring senior living communities in Alpharetta

  • Visit at mealtime to get a feel for daily life
  • Ask what is included in the monthly fee and what costs extra
  • Check the community's state license and inspection history
  • For 55+ rentals, ask which monthly fees are added on top of base rent
  • Tour at least three communities before you decide

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the best senior living communities in Alpharetta, GA?

Start with providers based in or regularly serving Alpharetta, check licensing and reviews, and get more than one quote for bigger jobs. This page lists 10 options for 55+ active adult apartments, independent living, assisted living and memory care communities.

How do I check if an assisted living community is licensed in Georgia?

Personal care homes and assisted living communities are licensed by the Georgia Department of Community Health's Healthcare Facility Regulation Division. Ask the community for its license and recent survey results, or contact the division.

What is the difference between a personal care home and assisted living in Georgia?

Under Georgia rules, an assisted living community is a personal care home with 25 or more residents that is licensed to provide assisted living care. Smaller personal care homes provide housing, meals and personal services.

Who can help if there is a problem at a senior care facility?

Georgia's Long Term Care Ombudsman Program investigates and helps resolve resident complaints. You can also file a complaint with the Department of Community Health.

Do these senior living communities serve all of Alpharetta?
