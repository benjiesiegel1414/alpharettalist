Choosing an auto body shop in Alpharetta: what to know

After a fender bender on GA 400 or in a busy parking lot, the body shop you pick determines how well your car is put back together. Georgia uses a fault based system: the at fault driver's liability insurance pays for damage to others, and the state's Office of Commissioner of Insurance notes you can also file under your own collision coverage even when someone else caused the crash. Ask your agent which route makes more sense for your situation before you choose a shop.

You do not have to use the shop your insurer suggests. Georgia law says an insurer may not require you to use a particular repair facility to settle a claim if you can get the work done at the same cost elsewhere, and it may not suggest that using or avoiding a shop could lead to nonpayment. Get a written estimate, ask whether the shop follows the manufacturer's repair procedures, and find out whether it plans to use original equipment, aftermarket or used parts.

Ask about the shop's warranty on paint and workmanship, how it handles supplemental damage found after teardown, and whether it can recalibrate cameras and sensors that newer cars rely on. Request photos during the repair and a final invoice listing parts and labor. Red flags include pressure to sign a direction to pay before you have seen an estimate, vague answers about parts, and reluctance to let you inspect the car before pickup. Report hit and run damage to police promptly, since your insurer may ask for the report.

Tips for hiring auto body shops in Alpharetta

  • In Georgia you can choose your own body shop, even if your insurer recommends one
  • Ask whether the shop uses OEM or aftermarket parts

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the best auto body shops in Alpharetta, GA?

Start with providers based in or regularly serving Alpharetta, check licensing and reviews, and get more than one quote for bigger jobs. This page lists 3 options for collision repair, dent repair, paint and insurance claims.

Can my insurance company make me use their body shop in Georgia?

No. Georgia law says an insurer may not require you to use a particular repair facility to settle a claim if you can get the repairs at the same cost elsewhere.

Is Georgia an at fault state for car accidents?

Yes. The at fault driver's liability coverage pays for others' damages, and you may also file under your own collision coverage if you carry it.

What parts will be used on my car?

Ask the shop and your insurer whether the estimate calls for original equipment, aftermarket or used parts. Get the answer in writing.
