Alpharetta Winter Parade 2026: Date, Time, Route and Road Closures

By Alpharetta List staffPublished November 20, 20266 min read

Alpharetta is getting a brand-new holiday tradition this year. The first Alpharetta Winter Parade, presented by the Rotary Club of Alpharetta, rolls down Canton Street on Saturday, Dec. 12, 2026, with school bands, youth groups, civic clubs, local businesses and Santa himself bringing up the rear. Here is everything you need to plan your night: the time, the route, which roads close, where to watch and what else is happening that weekend.

Quick answer: Alpharetta Winter Parade 2026

  • Date: Saturday, Dec. 12, 2026
  • Start time: 5:30 p.m. The Rotary Club's parade guidelines say it ends by 7:30 p.m. (the Awesome Alpharetta calendar lists 5:30 to 7 p.m.)
  • Start: Innovation Academy, 125 Milton Ave.
  • Finish: Alpharetta Community Center, 175 Roswell St., next to Wills Park
  • Route: about 1.5 miles, down Canton Street and Roswell Street through downtown Alpharetta
  • Cost: free to watch, no tickets
  • Road closures: 4 to 8 p.m. on parts of Old Milton Parkway, Canton Street/Roswell Street and Milton Avenue
  • No parking: enforced inside the closed area from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. on parade day
  • Santa: yes, a live Santa closes out the parade

What the Alpharetta Winter Parade is

The Rotary Club of Alpharetta describes the parade as a festive celebration of the community that highlights Alpharetta's youth, civic organizations and the club itself. It is the parade's first year, and it is run with the City of Alpharetta, according to Alpharetta Now's coverage of the club's letter to downtown residents and businesses.

Expect a hometown lineup rather than giant balloons. Eligible entries include school bands and student groups, scout troops and youth sports and dance teams, civic clubs like Rotary, Lions, Garden and Optimist, businesses that serve Alpharetta, city departments such as fire, EMS, parks and public works, horse and dog clubs, local arts and cultural groups, and neighborhood groups. Every entry has to carry a winter theme, and festive lights, decorations and holiday attire are strongly encouraged, so it should glow nicely after dark.

One fun detail: no entry may include anyone dressed as Santa or Mrs. Claus. Organizers are saving that for the grand finale, when a live Santa arrives at the end of the parade.

Parade route

The route is about 1.5 miles and runs north to south through the heart of downtown. Based on the Rotary Club's guidelines and Alpharetta Now's route summary, it goes:

  • From Innovation Academy (125 Milton Ave.) along School Drive
  • Right onto Canton Street, heading south through downtown past Milton Avenue
  • Down Roswell Street and across Old Milton Parkway
  • Into the Alpharetta Community Center at 175 Roswell St., by Wills Park

Because the parade must wrap by 7:30 p.m., there are no stops along the way. Bands and dance groups perform while moving, and public safety staff keep the entries rolling so roads can reopen quickly.

Road closures and parking

The Rotary Club's road closure notice for Saturday, Dec. 12 lists these roads as closed from 4 to 8 p.m.:

  • Old Milton Parkway from Highway 9 to Wills Road
  • Canton Street / Roswell Street from School Drive to Devore Road
  • Milton Avenue from Highway 9 to Wills Drive

The same notice says no parking within the closed area will be enforced from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. on parade day. If you live or work downtown, move your car before 6 a.m. Earlier notices covered by local outlets listed a 5:30 to 7:30 p.m. window, so go by the newer 4 to 8 p.m. notice and give yourself extra time.

The Old Milton Parkway closure is the big one for drivers. It cuts the main east-west road across the south end of downtown, so if you are just passing through between Highway 9 and Wills Road that evening, plan another way around.

The Rotary Club has not published dedicated spectator parking. Parade entries use the rear lot at Innovation Academy for drop-off starting at 4 p.m., so skip that lot. Your best bet is to park outside the closed streets, arrive early and walk in. Our downtown Alpharetta restaurant guide has notes on public parking decks if you are making a dinner of it.

Where to watch

Organizers have not named official viewing areas, so any safe spot along the curb on the route works. A few ideas:

  • Near the start on Canton Street: the north end of Canton Street near School Drive should see the parade first, so little ones can head home earlier.
  • Heart of downtown: the stretch of Canton Street around Milton Avenue puts you close to restaurants, coffee and restrooms. See the best restaurants in downtown Alpharetta to book a table before or after.
  • Near the finish on Roswell Street: watching near the Alpharetta Community Center and Wills Park means you are close to where Santa wraps up the night.

Marchers are allowed to hand out commercially wrapped candy, and the club asks them to walk beside floats and hand it to spectators. Organizers also ask families to keep kids from running toward floats and vehicles, so bring a bag for candy and pick a spot where children can stay on the curb.

Tips for parade night

  • Bundle up. It is an evening parade in mid-December, so bring layers, gloves and a blanket or folding chairs.
  • Arrive early. With closures starting at 4 p.m., getting downtown gets harder as the evening goes on.
  • Leave drones and confetti at home. The guidelines ban drones, confetti, glitter, silly string, fireworks and smoke devices during the event.
  • Check the weather. The Rotary Club and the City of Alpharetta can delay, change or cancel the parade for weather or safety, so check the parade page that afternoon.
  • Hold the flyers. Entries can show business names and logos, but nobody may hand out flyers, brochures or business cards along the route, and political campaigning is not allowed.

Want to march? Applications close Nov. 30

Groups that want to be in the parade can apply on the Rotary Club of Alpharetta's Winter Parade page. Applications are due by Nov. 30, 2026, every entry needs approval from the parade committee, and approved groups get a confirmation email. Load-in is 4 to 5 p.m. in the rear lot at Innovation Academy, and all entries must be lined up by 5 p.m. Floats and vehicles must be under 15 feet tall and 12 feet wide, and children under 6 must be with a parent or guardian.

More holiday fun that weekend

  • Makers Market at Avalon, Dec. 12: a free holiday street fair of local artists and makers earlier the same day at Avalon.
  • Lights on the Loop: the free light display on the Alpha Loop between Northwinds Parkway and Old Milton Parkway runs nightly from 5:30 to 11 p.m., Dec. 4 through Jan. 4.
  • Avalon on Ice: skate under the lights at Avalon. See our Avalon on Ice guide for hours and prices.
  • Christmas lights: our Christmas lights near Alpharetta roundup covers drive-through and walk-through displays.

Frequently asked questions

When is the Alpharetta Winter Parade 2026?

Saturday, Dec. 12, 2026. It starts at 5:30 p.m., and the Rotary Club of Alpharetta's guidelines say the parade must end by 7:30 p.m.

What is the Alpharetta Winter Parade route?

The roughly 1.5-mile route starts at Innovation Academy, 125 Milton Ave., follows School Drive to Canton Street, heads south on Canton and Roswell streets across Old Milton Parkway and ends at the Alpharetta Community Center, 175 Roswell St.

What roads close for the Alpharetta Winter Parade?

From 4 to 8 p.m. on Dec. 12: Old Milton Parkway from Highway 9 to Wills Road, Canton Street/Roswell Street from School Drive to Devore Road, and Milton Avenue from Highway 9 to Wills Drive. No parking is enforced in the closed area from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m.

Is the Alpharetta Winter Parade free?

Yes. There are no tickets or fees to watch the parade.

Will Santa be at the Alpharetta Winter Parade?

Yes. The Rotary Club of Alpharetta says a live Santa Claus will be at the end of the parade.

How do I enter the Alpharetta Winter Parade?

Apply on the Rotary Club of Alpharetta's Winter Parade page by Nov. 30, 2026. Every entry needs parade committee approval, and entries must carry a festive winter theme.
