Fulton County Schools Redistricting: What the Draft Maps Mean for Alpharetta Families

By Alpharetta List staffPublished November 9, 20266 min read

Fulton County Schools has released its first draft attendance zone maps for north Fulton, and the biggest change for Alpharetta families is on the middle school side: the draft would combine Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge middle schools into one replacement middle school. Nothing is final. The maps are a first draft, the district will revise them after this week's forums, and the school board isn't scheduled to vote until February 2027. Here's what the draft shows, the dates that matter, and how to weigh in.

The short version

  • What's under review: four middle schools (Autrey Mill, Elkins Pointe, Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge) and four elementary schools (Esther Jackson, Hembree Springs, Mimosa and Vickery Mill), all in what the district calls Region C, north of the Chattahoochee River.
  • Middle school draft: Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge students would be consolidated into a replacement middle school, and one area now zoned for Haynes Bridge would move to Autrey Mill. Elkins Pointe shows no changes.
  • Why: enrollment is falling. The district projects the four middle schools will go from 3,141 students this year to roughly 2,600 to 2,700 by 2030-31.
  • Next forums: elementary on Monday, Nov. 9 (6:30 to 8:30 p.m., Roswell High School gym) and middle school on Wednesday, Nov. 11 (6 to 8 p.m., Centennial High School gym).
  • Decision timeline: revised maps in December, a first read for the school board on Jan. 12, 2027, and a final vote on Feb. 25, 2027.

Why Fulton County Schools is redistricting

The short answer is fewer students. According to Appen Media, Fulton County Schools enrolled 83,164 students this school year, down more than 2,300 from last year, and Region C's elementary and middle schools enrolled 559 fewer students than the year before based on the 20-day count.

In September, the Fulton County Board of Education voted to move capacity and attendance zone planning forward in Region C (and in Region A, south of Atlanta). The district is clear that no boundary changes, closures or consolidations have been approved yet. Board policy allows schools in an underused area to be considered for closure, consolidation or repurposing when neighboring schools have room.

"With this decline in student population, many facilities in Region C will continue to be underutilized in the 2030-2031 school year," Lauren Malekebu, the district's executive director of operational planning, told Appen Media.

What the draft means for Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge

Both schools have Alpharetta addresses: Haynes Bridge Middle is at 10665 Haynes Bridge Road and Holcomb Bridge Middle is at 2700 Holcomb Bridge Road. They're also two of the least full middle schools in the review. The district's draft packet, dated Nov. 8, lists this year's 20-day enrollment as:

  • Haynes Bridge Middle: 570 students in a 1,000-seat building (57%)
  • Holcomb Bridge Middle: 530 students in a 1,000-seat building (53%)
  • Autrey Mill Middle: 1,220 students, 1,450 seats (84%)
  • Elkins Pointe Middle: 821 students, 1,200 seats (68%)

The draft proposes two changes:

  • Area 1: a section on the east side of the current Haynes Bridge zone would be redistricted to Autrey Mill Middle. Barnwell Elementary, which currently splits between Haynes Bridge and Autrey Mill, would feed fully into Autrey Mill. The district estimates that adds about 118 students to Autrey Mill by 2030-31.
  • Area 2: the rest of the Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge student population would be consolidated into a replacement middle school. The draft map marks a "New School Preferred Site" next to the current Holcomb Bridge Middle campus, with a planned capacity of 1,200.

The middle school packet lists Northwood, Hillside, River Eves, Esther Jackson, Vickery Mill and Mimosa elementary schools as feeders for the combined school, with Esther Jackson feeding it fully. The district says the maps may be adjusted before December, so feeder details could shift. The district's projections show 897 students for the two schools' combined zone in 2028-29 and about 825 students (69% of capacity) at the consolidated school by 2030-31.

Back in October, Chief Operations Officer Noel Maloof told Atlanta News First that the district was looking to consolidate down to three middle schools in this group, and that Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge could be closed and replaced. "We can't run our schools half full," he said, arguing that combining under-resourced schools means students get "all the resources they need, and we're not splitting it between two schools."

What the draft means for elementary schools

Several of the elementary schools in the review feed Haynes Bridge or Holcomb Bridge today, so families in those zones may want to look at both packets. The elementary draft moves four areas:

  • Vickery Mill to Hembree Springs
  • Mimosa to Vickery Mill
  • Vickery Mill to Esther Jackson
  • Mimosa to Esther Jackson

The enrollment table in the draft lists no future enrollment for Mimosa Elementary, and its students are split between Vickery Mill and Esther Jackson. Vickery Mill, at 36% full this year, would rise to a projected 67% in 2028-29.

What families are saying

The district has asked for feedback on the trade-offs, and families have raised real ones. At the first middle school forum in October, one grandparent with three future middle schoolers told Atlanta News First his biggest worry was traffic, pointing to Holcomb Bridge Road and Ga. 400 at rush hour and asking what happens if a school run starts taking 45 minutes.

District leaders say commute and neighborhood ties are part of the criteria. The school board's policy puts geographic proximity, instructional capacity and projected enrollment first, then weighs traffic patterns, feeder alignment, special programs and whether a neighborhood was rezoned in the last three years. "We want to keep those important pieces together for those communities," Maloof said in October.

Forum dates and how to give feedback

  • Elementary forum #2 (draft maps): Monday, Nov. 9, 6:30 to 8:30 p.m., Roswell High School gym, 11595 King Road.
  • Middle school forum #2 (draft maps): Wednesday, Nov. 11, 6 to 8 p.m., Centennial High School gym, 9310 Scott Road.
  • Online: the district plans to post a recording and a feedback survey the day after each forum (Nov. 10 for elementary, Nov. 12 for middle).
  • Revised maps: Dec. 14 (elementary) and Dec. 15 (middle), same times and locations.
  • School board: first read Jan. 12, 2027; final action Feb. 25, 2027.

You can also email comments to planning@fultonschools.org or call Operational Planning at 470-254-5540. If you need an interpreter at a forum, email districtlanguageassistance@fultonschools.org at least 72 hours ahead, or call 470-254-6827 for shorter notice. The maps and packets are on the district's redistricting page.

Frequently asked questions

Are Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge middle schools closing?

Not yet. Fulton County Schools' first draft map, released Nov. 8, 2026, would consolidate the two schools' students into a replacement middle school, with a preferred site next to the current Holcomb Bridge campus. It is only a draft. The district will revise it after community forums, and the school board's final vote is scheduled for Feb. 25, 2027.

Which schools are part of the Fulton County Schools Region C redistricting?

Four middle schools (Autrey Mill, Elkins Pointe, Haynes Bridge and Holcomb Bridge) and four elementary schools (Esther Jackson, Hembree Springs, Mimosa and Vickery Mill), all north of the Chattahoochee River.

When are the Fulton County Schools redistricting forums?

The draft map forums are Nov. 9, 2026 for elementary schools (6:30 to 8:30 p.m., Roswell High School gym) and Nov. 11 for middle schools (6 to 8 p.m., Centennial High School gym). Revised maps will be shown Dec. 14 (elementary) and Dec. 15 (middle) at the same places.

How can I give feedback on the Fulton County Schools draft maps?

Attend a forum, fill out the online survey the district posts the day after each forum, or email planning@fultonschools.org. You can also call the Operational Planning office at 470-254-5540.

Why is Fulton County Schools redistricting north Fulton?

Enrollment is falling. The district says many Region C buildings will stay underused through 2030-31. This year Haynes Bridge Middle is 57% full and Holcomb Bridge Middle is 53% full, based on the 20-day enrollment count.
