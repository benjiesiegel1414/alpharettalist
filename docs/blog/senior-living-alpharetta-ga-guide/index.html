Senior Living in Alpharetta, GA: 55+ Apartments, Assisted Living and Memory Care

By Alpharetta List staffPublished November 20, 20267 min read

Alpharetta has become one of north metro Atlanta's busiest places for senior living. In the last few years a wave of new 55+ rental communities has opened around downtown and north Alpharetta, joining established assisted living and memory care communities along Morris Road, North Point and Crabapple. Here is how the options compare, what they cost, and the questions to ask before you sign.

Quick answer: If you are healthy and want a social, low-maintenance place to live, look at 55+ active adult rentals (roughly $1,800 to $3,800 a month). If you want meals and services but no care, look at independent living. If a parent needs daily help with bathing, dressing or medications, compare assisted living (about $4,745 a month on average in Alpharetta). For Alzheimer's or other dementia, tour memory care. 55+ active adult rentals

This is the fastest-growing part of Alpharetta's senior market. These communities are ordinary rentals with an age requirement, built around a clubhouse and a social calendar, with no meal plan or care. Most are managed by Greystar, and most allow pets.

  • Amorance (2001 Commerce St) is the walkable choice: 249 apartments and townhomes beside a five-acre park in downtown Alpharetta, with a rooftop club room, pool and a yoga and spin room. You can walk to City Center restaurants and the Alpha Loop.
  • Everleigh Deerfield (1010 Meliora Rd) is a gated community in north Alpharetta with studios to two bedrooms, a clubhouse, pool, art workshop, library, gardens, a dog park and a dog wash. 12-month leases have been listed from about $1,900 a month including fees.
  • Emblem Alpharetta (1000 Fanfare Way) is gated with covered parking, an on-site hair and nail salon, an outdoor kitchen and a community garden, plus unusually flexible leases from 2 to 15 months.

When you compare, ask for the total monthly price, not just base rent. Most communities add required monthly fees for things like trash, pest control and amenities, which can add $100 or more.

Independent living

Independent living communities add the services that make daily life easier: chef-prepared meals, housekeeping, scheduled transportation and a full activity calendar. The big advantage of choosing one on a campus that also offers assisted living is that you do not have to move again if needs change.

  • Atria North Point (100 Somerby Dr) offers independent living, assisted living and memory care on 20 wooded acres with walking paths and a heated indoor pool.
  • Village Park Alpharetta (Morris Road) offers apartments, city homes, bungalows and cottages. Its main campus is licensed for assisted living, so an independent home can switch to assisted living without a move.

Assisted living

Assisted living is for seniors who need regular help with activities like bathing, dressing, getting around or managing medications, with staff on site around the clock. In Georgia, an assisted living community is a personal care home serving 25 or more residents that is licensed by the Department of Community Health to provide assisted living care.

  • Inspired Living at Alpharetta (Morris Road) is known for its Any Time Dining program, a pool and walking paths. Small pets up to 30 pounds are welcome.
  • Legacy Ridge at Alpharetta (4125 North Point Pkwy) advertises all-inclusive shared apartments from $3,500 a month and Alexa-enabled smart apartments.
  • Chapters Living of Alpharetta (762 N Main St), formerly Addington Place, offers assisted living and memory care close to downtown.
  • Arbor Terrace Crabapple (Crabapple Road) adds a Bridges program for seniors in the early stages of memory loss, between assisted living and full memory care.
  • The Phoenix at Milton (13943 Highway 9) has a bistro and pub, a salon, walking trails and a playground for visiting grandkids.

Memory care

Memory care is a secured neighborhood for people living with Alzheimer's disease or other forms of dementia, with staff trained in dementia care and a structured daily routine. Every assisted living community above also offers memory care. When you tour, ask about staff-to-resident ratios on each shift, how the community handles wandering and sundowning, and what activities fill a typical day.

Respite stays

Several Alpharetta communities offer short respite stays, which can run from a few days to several weeks. They are a good fit after a hospital stay, while a family caregiver travels, or as a trial run before a permanent move. Inspired Living, Legacy Ridge and The Phoenix at Milton all list respite care, and The Phoenix allows stays of up to 60 days.

How families pay for senior living

  • Private funds. Most residents pay from savings, retirement income or the sale of a home.
  • Long-term care insurance. Many policies cover assisted living and memory care. Check the elimination period and daily benefit.
  • Veterans benefits. The VA's Aid and Attendance benefit can add to a pension for eligible wartime veterans and surviving spouses who need help with daily activities.
  • Medicare generally does not pay for assisted living or long-term room and board, though it may cover a short skilled nursing or rehab stay after a hospital visit.
  • Medicaid. Georgia's Medicaid waiver programs can help some eligible seniors with care services. Ask each community whether it works with them.

Questions to ask on every tour

  1. What is the total monthly price, and what exactly is included?
  2. How are care fees assessed, and what triggers an increase?
  3. Is there a one-time community or move-in fee?
  4. How many staff are on each shift, and is a nurse on site?
  5. What happens if care needs grow beyond what you can provide?
  6. Can I see your state license and most recent inspection report?
  7. Can we stay for a meal or join an activity before deciding?

Georgia's Department of Community Health licenses personal care homes and assisted living communities, and the state's Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program helps residents and families resolve problems. Visit at mealtime, talk to residents, and tour at least three communities before you decide.

Frequently asked questions

How much does assisted living cost in Alpharetta, GA?

A Place for Mom puts the average cost of assisted living in Alpharetta at about $4,745 a month, close to the Georgia average of $4,752 and below the national average of $5,909. Memory care usually costs more.

Are there 55+ apartments in Alpharetta?

Yes. Alpharetta has several 55+ active adult rental communities, including Amorance in downtown Alpharetta, Everleigh Deerfield and Emblem Alpharetta. Rents generally run about $1,800 to $3,800 a month.

What is the difference between independent living and assisted living?

Independent living includes an apartment plus services like meals, housekeeping and transportation, but no hands-on care. Assisted living adds daily help with bathing, dressing and medications, with staff on site around the clock.

Does Medicare pay for assisted living?

Generally no. Medicare does not cover long-term assisted living or room and board, though it may cover a short skilled nursing or rehab stay after a hospital visit. Families usually pay privately or with long-term care insurance or veterans benefits.

How do I check an assisted living community's license in Georgia?

Assisted living communities and personal care homes are licensed by the Georgia Department of Community Health. Ask the community for its license and recent inspection report, or contact the department.
