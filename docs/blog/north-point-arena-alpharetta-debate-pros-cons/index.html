The North Point Arena Debate: The Case For and Against

By Alpharetta List staffPublished November 13, 20266 min read

Few decisions have split Alpharetta like the plan to turn North Point Mall into an entertainment district with a 20,000-seat arena. The City Council approved the zoning 4-3 in a meeting that ran past six hours and into the early morning of August 25, 2026, with Mayor Jim Gilvin casting the deciding vote. Supporters see a once-in-a-generation chance; critics worry about density, traffic and what Alpharetta becomes. Here are the facts and the strongest arguments on each side, so you can make up your own mind.

The facts

  • Who: Jamestown, the developer behind Ponce City Market, working with property owner New York Life and The Machete Group, which advises on the arena.
  • Where: the North Point Mall property, up to about 100 acres off GA 400 at exit 9.
  • What: as presented at a June 2026 town hall, a 20,000-seat arena designed for an NHL team, a 2,000-seat community hockey rink, a 4,000-seat music hall, a 500-seat movie theater, a 400-room full-service hotel and a 300-room hybrid hotel, a 45,000-square-foot conference center, 750,000 square feet of office space and 1,385 for-rent apartments.
  • The vote: 4-3 to approve the zoning on August 25, 2026. Council member Katie Reeves voted against; Council member John Hipes and Mayor Gilvin spoke in favor. The council changed its rules that night to allow unlimited public comment.
  • The big condition: a major part of the deal depends on the NHL committing to bring a team to Alpharetta by August 2031. The NHL has made no commitment to a team in metro Atlanta.
  • The competition: a rival project in Forsyth County, The Gathering at South Forsyth, about seven miles away, is also pitching an arena of more than 18,000 seats for an NHL team. Only one location can win.

For the full project rundown, see our North Point Mall redevelopment explainer.

The case for

A dying mall gets a second life. Jamestown says the mall has lost considerable property value, and supporters argue that doing nothing is not a real option. Even residents who are skeptical of the arena often agree the site needs something new.

It could work even without hockey. At the June town hall, David Carlock of The Machete Group said the district could still succeed with other uses if an NHL team goes elsewhere. The music hall, hotels, offices and restaurants are not tied to a franchise.

Southern hockey can work. Carlock pointed to the Tampa Bay Lightning and Carolina Hurricanes as proof that hockey can thrive in the South, and to Alpharetta's growth as a market.

Investment and attention. Supporters told the council the project could bring investment, families and national attention to Alpharetta. Council member Hipes called it an opportunity too great to pass up and compared it to the Avalon development.

A familiar developer. Fans of the plan point to Jamestown's track record with Ponce City Market. Jamestown also says it plans to keep the mall's beloved carousel, a nostalgic detail that resonated with longtime residents.

The case against

Density. The most common objection is size. More than 1,300 apartments, two hotels and a 20,000-seat arena on one site is a lot for a suburb. One speaker who supported redeveloping the mall told the council, "I just think this project is too dense and I don't see any parking." Council member Reeves asked whether "this kind of density" fits and, as she put it, "Will we still be Alpharetta?"

Traffic and parking. North Point Parkway, Haynes Bridge Road and GA 400 already back up at rush hour, and GA 400 is in the middle of a years-long express lanes project. Critics worry about game-night traffic layered on top.

Hockey's history in Atlanta. At the town hall, resident Francene Taylor noted that the Atlanta Flames drew modest crowds in the 1970s and that the Thrashers left for Winnipeg in 2011, and asked, "What has changed that makes you think this will be successful?"

Taxpayer risk. Opponents raised concerns about possible public funding. News coverage of the vote did not detail any public financing, and that remains one of the questions residents most want answered as the project moves forward.

Competition with Avalon and downtown. One resident worried that new shops and restaurants would pull customers from Avalon and Downtown Alpharetta. Jamestown's Frances Bohn said the tenant mix would complement, not compete with, existing businesses.

What happens next

The zoning vote lets the project keep moving, but it is not a guarantee of anything. Several hurdles remain, the biggest being an NHL decision that will be made outside Alpharetta. Watch for these milestones:

  • Any NHL announcement on expansion or relocation to metro Atlanta.
  • Progress, or setbacks, at the competing Forsyth County site.
  • Details on financing, phasing and traffic plans as the project goes through the city's development process.
  • The August 2031 deadline for an NHL commitment tied to the deal.

Frequently asked questions

Did Alpharetta approve the North Point Mall arena?

Yes. The Alpharetta City Council approved the zoning for the North Point Mall redevelopment 4-3 on August 25, 2026, with Mayor Jim Gilvin casting the deciding vote.

Is an NHL team coming to Alpharetta?

Not yet. The NHL has made no commitment to a team in metro Atlanta. A major part of the deal depends on the NHL committing to a team in Alpharetta by August 2031, and a rival arena project in Forsyth County is competing for the same team.

How many apartments are in the North Point plan?

Plans presented at a June 2026 town hall included 1,385 for-rent apartments, alongside a 20,000-seat arena, a music hall, two hotels, a conference center and 750,000 square feet of offices.

Why did people oppose the North Point arena?

Critics raised density, traffic, parking, possible taxpayer funding, hockey's track record in Atlanta and competition with Avalon and downtown. Supporters cited investment, a second life for a declining mall and national attention.
