Big Creek Greenway Guide: Where to Park, Best Stretches and Tips in Alpharetta

By Alpharetta List staffPublished November 8, 20265 min read

The Big Creek Greenway is Alpharetta's favorite place to walk, run and ride, and late fall is one of the best times to go, with cool mornings and the creek corridor turning gold. Here's a local's guide to where to park, which stretch to pick, how far it is between access points and a few rules worth knowing before you head out.

Big Creek Greenway at a glance

  • What it is: a 12-foot-wide paved multi-use path that follows Big Creek through wooded stream corridors, with boardwalk sections over the wetter spots.
  • Who uses it: walkers, runners, cyclists, inline skaters, dog walkers and families with strollers. Discover Georgia Outdoors notes the trail has no at-grade road crossings, which makes it a good pick for wheelchairs and strollers.
  • Cost: free.
  • Where: the Alpharetta section runs from Mansell Road and Big Creek Park in the south to Marconi Drive in the north, then continues into Roswell to the south. A separate Forsyth County section starts farther north at McFarland Parkway.
  • Phone: Alpharetta Recreation, Parks and Cultural Services, 678-297-6123.

You'll see different mileage numbers depending on who's counting. Discover Georgia Outdoors describes the main Alpharetta spine as an 8.3-mile linear trail, Awesome Alpharetta says "approximately 9 linear miles," and the city's economic development site, Connected Alpharetta, says Alpharetta now has 12 miles of Greenway trails. The short answer: the main north-south path is about 8 miles, and the city's total grows when you count its spurs and connectors. Our Big Creek Greenway listing keeps the basics in one place.

Where to park: Alpharetta access points

Awesome Alpharetta lists these parking and access locations for the Alpharetta segment:

  • Rock Mill Park on Kimball Bridge Road, roughly the halfway point of the trail, with restrooms, a pavilion and plenty of parking. It's our pick for first-timers. See Rock Mill Park.
  • Preston Ridge, by the Alpharetta Family YMCA, with restrooms.
  • Northpoint Park, convenient if you're coming from the North Point area or the mall.
  • Haynes Bridge Road and Kimball Bridge access areas.
  • Marconi Drive at the northern end.
  • Union Hill Park near the Forsyth County line. See Union Hill Park.
  • Beaver Creek Park, which is trail access only, so plan to walk or bike in.

Wikipedia's list of access points also flags a few spots with no public Greenway parking, including Old Milton Parkway, Mansell Crossing and the Barrington Farms neighborhood. If you want restrooms, start at Rock Mill, Preston Ridge or Big Creek Park on the Roswell end.

The best stretch for a first visit: Rock Mill to Marconi

Discover Georgia Outdoors recommends starting at Rock Mill Park and heading north, and it's a great introduction. Take the access trail from the northeast side of the parking lot near the restrooms, then turn left onto the Greenway. Here's how the distances break down, according to that guide:

  • Rock Mill to the Old Milton Parkway underpass: about 1.8 miles through quiet lowlands on the west side of the creek.
  • On to the Alpharetta Family YMCA: another 0.5 miles. This is a natural turnaround for families with small kids, since it's an out-and-back trail and every mile out is a mile back.
  • YMCA to Webb Bridge Road: about 0.7 miles of well-shaded forest, then 0.2 miles alongside the road before the trail passes under it.
  • The final push to Marconi Drive: 0.8 miles with a climb of about 125 feet.

That's 4 miles one way from Rock Mill to Marconi, or about 8 miles round trip. Walkers who want something shorter can do Rock Mill to the YMCA and back for a 4.6-mile outing. If you're near the north end anyway, the lake and arboretum trail at Webb Bridge Park make a nice add-on.

What you'll see along the way

Because the trail follows the Big Creek habitat, it feels a lot farther from the city than it is. Discover Georgia Outdoors says users regularly spot wild turkeys, green herons and deer, and Awesome Alpharetta mentions birds, mammals and plenty of plant life along the creek. There are also a few nature trails branching off the main path. They aren't counted in the official mileage, but they're worth a detour if you have time.

Rules and tips before you go

  • Speed limit: Discover Georgia Outdoors notes a 15 mph maximum for bikes. Weekend mornings get busy, so it's not the place for training sprints.
  • Helmets: Greenway rules require cyclists and skaters to wear helmets.
  • Rain and flooding: parts of the trail pass through marshy areas and can flood after heavy rain, closing some sections. Give it a day or two after a big storm.
  • Bring your own bike: the old Zagster bike-share stations along the trail are gone. Wikipedia notes that program was discontinued in May 2020.
  • It's an out-and-back: pick your turnaround point before you start, especially with kids or as the days get shorter in November.

Going farther: Roswell and Forsyth County

The Greenway doesn't stop at the city limits. To the south it continues past Mansell Road to Big Creek Park in Roswell. To the north, Forsyth County's section runs from McFarland Parkway up to Bethelview Road, with access at Fowler Park near Highway 9, according to Awesome Alpharetta. The Big Creek Greenway's own map page says the Alpharetta and Forsyth pieces were built as separate sections, with plans to link them over time.

Prefer something more urban? The Alpha Loop connects Downtown, Avalon, North Point and Northwinds. Connected Alpharetta says the Loop is now more than 6 miles long, with a North Point Mall connection under construction and a future link to the Big Creek Greenway planned. That will eventually let you go from a creekside forest walk to dinner in Downtown Alpharetta without getting in the car.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Big Creek Greenway in Alpharetta?

The main Alpharetta section runs about 8 miles from Mansell Road and Big Creek Park north to Marconi Drive. Discover Georgia Outdoors lists it at 8.3 miles, Awesome Alpharetta says about 9 linear miles, and the city's Connected Alpharetta site counts 12 miles of Greenway trails in Alpharetta when spurs and connectors are included.

Where is the best place to park for the Big Creek Greenway?

Rock Mill Park on Kimball Bridge Road is a good first stop. It sits near the middle of the trail and has parking, restrooms and a pavilion. Other access points include Preston Ridge by the Alpharetta Family YMCA, Northpoint Park, Haynes Bridge Road, Marconi Drive and Union Hill Park. Beaver Creek Park is trail access only.

Where are the restrooms on the Big Creek Greenway?

Restrooms are at Rock Mill Park (the Kimball Bridge parking area), by the Alpharetta Family YMCA at Preston Ridge, and at the southern end of Big Creek Park in Roswell.

Can you bike on the Big Creek Greenway?

Yes. The 12-foot-wide paved path is open to cyclists, walkers, runners and skaters. Bikes are limited to 15 mph, and Greenway rules require cyclists and skaters to wear helmets.

Does the Big Creek Greenway connect to Forsyth County?

Forsyth County has its own section of the Big Creek Greenway that runs from McFarland Parkway north to Bethelview Road, with access at Fowler Park. The Alpharetta and Forsyth sections were built separately, with plans to link them over time.
